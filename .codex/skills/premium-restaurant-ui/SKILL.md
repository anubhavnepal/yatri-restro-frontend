# Premium Restaurant UI

## When to use this skill

- When planning or reviewing the visual direction of public-facing pages and sections.
- When deciding how the restaurant brand should feel across hero areas, menu showcases, reservations, and CTAs.
- When a UI needs to feel more premium, calm, cinematic, and intentional.

## Project-specific rules

- Mobile-first design.
- Premium dark luxury aesthetic.
- Elegant spacing and typography.
- Accessible contrast and focus states.
- Clear CTAs.
- Smooth but subtle animation.
- Avoid heavy WebGL, particles, excessive motion, or performance-heavy visual effects.
- The brand is a Nepali cuisine restaurant in Japan, so the UI should balance cultural warmth with refined Japanese luxury restraint.
- Prioritize cinematic presentation for hospitality content without sacrificing clarity or usability.
- Keep layouts customer-facing and conversion-oriented for discovery, reservations, and orders.

## Expected output quality

- Feels premium and restaurant-specific rather than template-like.
- Uses spacing, type, imagery placement, and hierarchy to create trust and appetite appeal.
- Maintains a polished experience on small screens first.
- Supports accessibility and performance without losing atmosphere.

## Things to avoid

- Generic SaaS styling or loud promotional clutter.
- Flat, lifeless spacing or inconsistent typography rhythm.
- Weak CTAs that disappear into the background.
- Decorative motion that competes with content.
- Bright or playful aesthetics that conflict with the luxury dining direction.

## Checklist before completing work

- Confirm the visual direction matches premium Japanese luxury dining.
- Confirm the mobile view remains elegant and usable.
- Confirm CTAs are visible, purposeful, and easy to reach.
- Confirm contrast, focus states, and readability remain strong.
- Confirm motion remains subtle and lightweight.
