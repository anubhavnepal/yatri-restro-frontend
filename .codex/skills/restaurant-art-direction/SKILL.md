# Restaurant Art Direction

## When to use this skill

- When a public-facing restaurant page feels too generic, product-like, or dashboard-like.
- When planning or reviewing homepage, hero, featured dish, gallery, reservation CTA, or editorial landing-page work.
- When Codex needs to act like a senior restaurant website art director before implementing visual changes.
- When a layout technically works but lacks appetite, atmosphere, emotional hierarchy, or memorable identity.

## Premium restaurant art direction

- Design for desire, atmosphere, and trust before utility density.
- Treat the restaurant like a destination, not like a software product with feature panels.
- Prioritize emotionally legible section hierarchy over equal-weight modular grids.
- Let the homepage feel authored and composed, not assembled from interchangeable blocks.
- Make the guest imagine arrival, dining, and mood, not just navigation and flows.

## Food-first visual hierarchy

- Food should lead the first impression whenever strong dish imagery exists.
- Lead with signature dishes or plated moments before secondary informational content.
- Let cuisine visuals drive appetite and brand identity while support copy frames the experience.
- Keep service, ambience, and utility content as support layers rather than the homepage’s loudest elements.
- If a hero or section must choose between food and abstract atmosphere, food wins unless the brief explicitly says otherwise.

## Noir fine-dining mood

- Aim for a dark, cinematic, evening-led atmosphere with controlled contrast and warm highlights.
- Surfaces should feel lacquered, shadowed, and refined rather than flat, bright, or playful.
- Use darkness as stagecraft, not as opacity abuse.
- Keep the mood intimate and composed, like a quiet late-evening dining room.
- Avoid “club,” “gaming,” or “luxury tech” visual language.

## Muted gold accent usage

- Use muted gold to guide attention, not to decorate every edge.
- Best uses:
  - eyebrows
  - key dividers
  - selective CTA emphasis
  - quiet highlights around premium moments
- Avoid turning gold into a loud brand flood.
- Gold should feel brushed, restrained, and warm rather than glittery or saturated.

## Japanese luxury restraint with Nepali warmth

- Balance Japanese composure with Nepali hospitality and culinary warmth.
- Japanese restraint should shape pacing, spacing, refinement, and quiet confidence.
- Nepali warmth should come through food imagery, welcome language, hospitality cues, and tactile richness.
- Avoid overly cold minimalism that strips away humanity.
- Avoid overly rustic or noisy styling that breaks the premium dining tone.

## Avoiding generic bento/card/dashboard layouts

- Do not default to equal-height cards, equally weighted panels, or dashboard grids.
- Avoid homepage structures where every section looks like the same container repeated.
- Break rhythm intentionally: wide hero, tighter dish grid, slower featured story, quieter reservation band, lighter gallery cadence.
- Editorial composition beats utility uniformity.
- If a page resembles a SaaS marketing site, the art direction is failing.

## Creating editorial section rhythm

- Vary density and pacing across sections.
- Use one or two dominant sections, then support them with tighter secondary sections.
- Let some sections breathe with larger imagery and fewer decisions.
- Let other sections compress into sharper conversion moments.
- Alternate between appetite, atmosphere, and action so the page has narrative movement.
- Avoid giving every section identical spacing, weight, and visual tempo.

## Using imagery as storytelling, not decoration

- Every image should contribute to appetite, room tone, or hospitality.
- Use imagery to reveal dish identity, plating detail, atmosphere, or service rhythm.
- Do not add images just to “fill space.”
- Prefer fewer stronger images over many weak ones.
- Choose crops that honor food shape, texture, and plating.
- Give room images narrative purpose: arrival, warmth, lighting, table setting, or evening mood.

## Hero composition rules

- The hero must communicate restaurant identity instantly.
- Prefer strong asymmetry over a centered brochure panel when the brand needs more drama.
- When food imagery is available, make the hero food-first.
- Hero copy should establish mood, cuisine, and invitation in a clear hierarchy:
  - eyebrow
  - strong H1
  - short emotional support copy
  - primary CTA
  - secondary CTA
- Avoid stuffing hero space with utility notes, status labels, or too many actions.
- Do not let the hero become a generic product preview strip.

## Featured dish presentation rules

- Featured dishes should feel curated, not like a raw product catalog.
- Use stronger image presence than standard menu cards when on the homepage.
- Limit count so the section feels deliberate.
- Dish names, category, and price should support appetite rather than create metadata clutter.
- If add-to-cart is present, it must feel subordinate to the editorial presentation, not turn the section into a storefront grid.
- Featured dishes should look like signature recommendations, not equal commodity tiles.

## Reservation CTA mood rules

- Reservation should feel like an invitation into the evening, not a conversion funnel block.
- Use language around pace, welcome, table, timing, and arrival.
- Keep backend-owned truth clear, but do not let disclaimers dominate the emotional tone.
- The section should feel calm, elegant, and confidence-building.
- Avoid urgency gimmicks, fake scarcity, or hard-sell framing.

## Gallery and ambience storytelling rules

- Gallery previews should extend the restaurant story, not become random image dumps.
- Mix food, ambience, plating, and service detail when possible.
- Compose gallery sections with lead/support relationships rather than equal tiles only.
- Use captions sparingly and only when they add meaning.
- Avoid making the gallery feel like a CMS grid or stock photo wall.

## Accessibility and readability constraints

- Premium styling must never compromise readability.
- Maintain strong contrast for copy over dark surfaces.
- Avoid text overlays on complex imagery unless the overlay treatment is extremely reliable.
- Keep focus states visible and intentional.
- Preserve meaningful alt text and semantic section structure.
- Design for English and Japanese line lengths and wrapping behavior from the start.
- Decorative composition must never break reading order on mobile.

## Performance constraints for images

- Use imagery generously only when it remains optimized and intentional.
- Prioritize the most important image; lazy-load the rest.
- Prefer server-rendered page composition and minimal client-side behavior.
- Choose aspect ratios that stabilize layout and reduce jank.
- Avoid image-heavy concepts that require carousels, autoplay, or large client bundles just to feel premium.

## Things to avoid

- Equal-weight dashboard panels
- Generic SaaS cards
- Overused glassmorphism
- Fake discounts or coupons
- Visual clutter
- Unreadable overlays
- Layout tricks that risk bugs
- Decorative motion that overpowers content
- Overly clever collage layouts that become fragile on tablet or mobile
- Making every section dark, boxed, and identical in rhythm

## Checklist before implementing restaurant UI

- Does this look like a restaurant, not a dashboard?
- Does food imagery lead the experience?
- Is there a clear visual hierarchy?
- Are sections varied in rhythm?
- Are CTAs emotionally placed, not just mechanically placed?
- Is the design premium without being flashy?
- Is the layout stable on mobile, tablet, and desktop?
