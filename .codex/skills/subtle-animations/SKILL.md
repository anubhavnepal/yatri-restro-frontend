# Subtle Animations

## When to use this skill

- When defining motion behavior for hero sections, reveals, hover states, drawers, or transitions.
- When reviewing whether animations fit the restaurant’s premium tone.
- When deciding if motion adds clarity, delight, or conversion value.

## Project-specific rules

- Mobile-first design.
- Premium dark luxury aesthetic.
- Elegant spacing and typography.
- Accessible contrast and focus states.
- Clear CTAs.
- Smooth but subtle animation.
- Avoid heavy WebGL, particles, excessive motion, or performance-heavy visual effects.
- Favor short, calm motion that supports reading flow and product focus.
- Motion should reinforce premium hospitality, not imitate gaming or entertainment sites.
- Any animation strategy must respect reduced-motion preferences.

## Expected output quality

- Motion feels refined, quiet, and intentional.
- Animations support the hierarchy of content rather than distract from it.
- Performance remains strong on mobile devices.
- The user experience still works cleanly with motion reduced or disabled.

## Things to avoid

- Long, dramatic transitions that slow down core actions.
- Parallax or motion gimmicks that compromise readability.
- Layered animation stacks that create jank.
- Relying on motion to explain critical information.
- Ignoring reduced-motion accessibility expectations.

## Checklist before completing work

- Confirm motion supports the premium brand tone.
- Confirm animation timing and distance are subtle.
- Confirm reduced-motion behavior is accounted for.
- Confirm the plan avoids performance-heavy effects.
- Confirm CTAs and content remain the visual priority.
